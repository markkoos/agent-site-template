## 1. Deposit earnest money (first few days)

Your contract states how much and how fast — often within a few business days. The money goes to the escrow or title company, not the seller.

## 2. Inspections and disclosures (first 1–2 weeks)

Schedule a general home inspection right away, plus any specialists the inspector recommends (roof, sewer line, pest). Read the seller's disclosures carefully alongside the reports.

## 3. Request repairs or credits

Based on what you find, you can ask the seller to repair items, give a credit, or lower the price. Credits are often simpler than repairs because you control the work after closing.

## 4. Appraisal and loan approval (weeks 2–4)

Your lender orders an appraisal and works through underwriting. Avoid new credit, big purchases or job changes until you close.

## 5. Remove contingencies

Each contingency has a deadline. Once you remove one, you generally lose the right to cancel for that reason, so this is the moment to be sure.

## 6. Final walkthrough and closing (last few days)

Confirm agreed repairs were done and the home is in the expected condition. Then you sign loan and transfer documents, the loan funds, and the deed records. Keys usually follow the same day or as the contract specifies.

| Step | Typical timing |
|---|---|
| Earnest money | Within ~3 business days |
| Inspections | Days 1–10 |
| Appraisal | Days 7–21 |
| Loan approval | Days 14–30 |
| Closing | Days 30–45 |
