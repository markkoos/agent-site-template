## The problem with price per square foot

Automated valuations and quick comparative analyses lean on price per square foot within a tight radius. That works for tract homes that are broadly similar. It breaks down for a house whose value lives in its design: the plan, the light, the materials, the relationship to the site.

## What actually drives value

- **Design pedigree.** Who designed it, and how intact is the original vision?
- **Condition of original details.** Restored versus remuddled can be the difference between a premium and a discount.
- **Setting.** Views, privacy and how the house meets its land.
- **Livability.** Updated systems and kitchens that respect the architecture.

## Building a better comp set

1. Look beyond the immediate neighborhood to sales of comparable architecture.
2. Extend the time window and adjust for market movement since each sale.
3. Separate the land value from the structure where possible.
4. Talk to the agents involved — the numbers rarely tell you why a house sold above or below expectations.

## The takeaway for sellers

Ask any agent you interview to show you *which* comparables they used and *why*. If the answer is a radius search sorted by square footage, keep looking.
